# Social Media Strategy & Reach Optimization

Read this when the user wants captions, descriptions, hashtags, a publishing plan or a "strategy" for a piece of content, or whenever you deliver copy with an asset. The goal is more than good captions: give every piece of content the best chance of organic reach, search discovery, shares, saves, meaningful comments and profile visits. Work like an agency that runs high-performing accounts.

Writing rules, per-platform deliverables and hashtag rules are in `copywriting.md`. This file covers **research → analysis → publishing strategy → delivery**.

## Platform scope
- **Always ask the user which platforms they want** before starting (see `SKILL.md` §1), unless they already named them in the request. Supported: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, YouTube. Never assume a default set.
- Cover exactly the platforms the user chose — no more, no fewer.
- If the content has no sensible format on a platform (e.g. a 4:5 feed carousel on YouTube), say so in that platform's section and suggest the closest fit (Community post, convert it to a Short…). Don't force it.
- The output format in §5 lists all six; include only the sections for the chosen platforms.

## 1. Research before writing (mandatory)
Use web search when it is available. Research must be real:

1. **Topic:** what the content is about, the terms people actually use for it, and any facts you'll state.
2. **Audience:** who it is for, what they care about, what problem or desire it touches.
3. **Search keywords:** phrases people type into TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and Google search. Platform search suggestions and the titles of top-ranking videos are good evidence.
4. **Current trends** on the exact topic: formats, sounds, angles, news. Note the source and date.
5. **Hashtags:** check that each one is actually used for this kind of content on that platform (see `copywriting.md` §C).
6. **Competitors and successful creators** in the niche: hooks, formats, title patterns, what gets comments.
7. **Platform rules:** current limits and guideline changes (hashtag caps, character limits, policies).

**Never:**
- invent trending hashtags, or call a hashtag "trending" without verifying it;
- invent statistics, view counts, engagement rates or "best time" data;
- pick a popular hashtag that doesn't fit the content;
- assume a TikTok trend suits LinkedIn;
- present outdated advice or algorithm folklore as current fact.

**If live research isn't possible or turns up nothing,** say so plainly ("trend data unverified — no live search available") and fall back to evergreen SEO: descriptive keywords, niche and topic hashtags, and audience relevance. Keep **verified** facts and **unverified** suggestions visibly separate.

## 2. Content analysis
Before writing, answer these about the actual content:
- **Stop-scroll:** what in the first second or first line makes someone stop?
- **Curiosity:** what open question keeps them watching or reading?
- **Completion:** why would they stay to the end? Is there a payoff, a reveal or a result?
- **Share trigger:** who would they send this to, and why? ("this is so you", "you need this", "look what they did")
- **Save trigger:** is there reusable value, such as steps, a checklist, a template or a reference?
- **Emotion or problem:** what does it make people feel, or which problem does it solve?

Choose techniques that fit the content honestly: curiosity gap · pattern interrupt · storytelling · unexpected insight · relatable situation · educational value · strong visual hook · before/after · problem → solution · a contrarian but **factual** viewpoint.
**No misleading clickbait.** The hook must be paid off by the content. No false claims, no fake urgency.

## 3. Publishing strategy
After writing the six platform versions, give:
- **Best platform** for this specific content, and why.
- **Publishing order** and the reasoning behind it (e.g. TikTok first to test the hook, then Reels/Shorts with the stronger variant, then LinkedIn/X with the takeaway).
- **Format per platform** (Reel vs feed post vs carousel, Short vs long-form, native video vs link post).
- **Posting times:** only when the user's analytics or a cited, recent source supports them. Otherwise say "check your account's insights for when your audience is online", and don't invent hours.
- **Best CTA** for this content, plus the engagement plan (pinned comment, replying to early comments, Story or Community follow-up).
- **One alternative hook** to test.
- **One A/B test** that changes a single variable (hook, cover text, title, or the first line of the caption).

**Account data:** don't invent the user's audience analytics. Label recommendations as **general** (from research or best practice) or **account-specific** (from data the user gave you).

## 4. Language
Choose one publishing language from the content, the target market, the brand and the platform audience, and use it on every platform. Use a different language on one platform only when there's a strategic reason, and state the reason. Never translate the same caption into several languages by default. (Writing rules for each language are in `copywriting.md` §A and §D.)

## 6. Final quality check (do it, then report it)
- [ ] Every platform the user chose is covered (and only those)
- [ ] Every platform has its own copy: no copy-paste, and no opening line repeated across platforms
- [ ] Each version fits its platform's tone and format
- [ ] Hashtags are relevant, verified or marked unverified, never fabricated; counts are within the limits in `copywriting.md` §C
- [ ] Keywords read naturally, with no stuffing
- [ ] No generic AI phrasing (`copywriting.md` §A); CTAs aren't repeated word-for-word
- [ ] Character limits respected; X posts counted
- [ ] No unsupported trend, algorithm, statistic or posting-time claims
- [ ] No invented facts, offers, URLs, phone numbers or testimonials
- [ ] Research sources listed truthfully

If anything fails, fix it before delivering. **Never deliver lazy, generic or copy-pasted content.**
